## Platform/Data-Source Architecture

- The 2026-02-18 multi-platform research states that Chapa's scoring pipeline is platform-agnostic and that GitHub coupling lives in GraphQL query and OAuth layers. Reference: `docs/research/multi-platform.md:9`.
- The Bitbucket research states that the scoring pipeline has zero GitHub-specific logic and that platform coupling lived in the GraphQL query, fetch function, and OAuth flow at that time. Reference: `docs/research/2026-02-23-bitbucket-integration.md:9`.
- The Bitbucket research states that `SupplementalStats` plus `mergeStats()` already demonstrated multi-source aggregation and that Bitbucket data would be transformed into `StatsData` and merged with GitHub data. Reference: `docs/research/2026-02-23-bitbucket-integration.md:11`.
- The Bitbucket plan states that GitHub login remains unchanged, Bitbucket is optional, Bitbucket data is fetched server-side through OAuth, transformed into `StatsData`, and merged with GitHub data through `mergeStats()`. Reference: `docs/plans/2026-02-23-bitbucket-integration.md:9`.
- The Bitbucket plan states that scoring, badge rendering, and downstream consumers remain unchanged for that integration. Reference: `docs/plans/2026-02-23-bitbucket-integration.md:9`.
- The Codeberg plan states that Codeberg follows the Bitbucket integration pattern: GitHub login remains primary, Codeberg is linked from the User Menu, Codeberg stats are fetched through OAuth, merged with GitHub data, and reflected in the badge. Reference: `docs/plans/2026-02-26-codeberg-integration.md:10`.
- The GitLab plan states that GitLab is added as a fourth connectable developer source, with commit/MR/review data merged into `StatsData` and used by Impact v6 scoring. Reference: `docs/plans/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:8`.
- The GitLab plan states that GitLab users link/unlink from the User Menu and that the GitLab logo appears in badge branding for connected users. Reference: `docs/plans/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:8`.
- The GitLab research states that Bitbucket and Codeberg were built on a shared OAuth factory and a single `Platform` union type, while scoring, badge SVG rendering, and impact specs were platform-agnostic. Reference: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:11`.
- The GitLab plan states that `lib/auth/platform-oauth.ts` did not change because its `PlatformOAuthConfig` and four handler factories already accepted any platform string. Reference: `docs/plans/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:18`.

## Link/Unlink and Storage

- The Bitbucket research states that Bitbucket is a linked account rather than primary login: users log in with GitHub, link Bitbucket, fetch Bitbucket data with the linked token, and merge data through `mergeStats()`. References: `docs/research/2026-02-23-bitbucket-integration.md:294`, `docs/research/2026-02-23-bitbucket-integration.md:296`.
- The Bitbucket research states that this pattern keeps the GitHub handle as canonical identity. Reference: `docs/research/2026-02-23-bitbucket-integration.md:302`.
- The Bitbucket plan records Supabase `user_platforms` as the storage choice because Bitbucket tokens expire and Redis flushes should not remove the link. Reference: `docs/plans/2026-02-23-bitbucket-integration.md:18`.
- The GitLab research states that connection state is persisted in Supabase `user_platforms`, with `dbGetLinkedPlatform`, `dbUpsertLinkedPlatform`, `dbDeleteLinkedPlatform`, `dbUpdatePlatformTokens`, `dbHasLinkedPlatform`, and `dbGetLinkedPlatforms`. References: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:77`, `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:79`, `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:80`, `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:81`.
- The GitLab research states that `user_platforms` stores lowercased handle, platform, remote login, access token, refresh token, expiry, connected/updated timestamps, and id; tokens are encrypted with AES-256-GCM keyed by `NEXTAUTH_SECRET`. Reference: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:84`.
- The GitLab research states that `user_platforms` is keyed by `(handle, platform)` and required no schema migration for GitLab. References: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:86`, `docs/plans/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:15`.
- The GitLab research states that Redis stores stats caches rather than connection state, cookies store only short-lived CSRF state, OAuth tokens are not stored in cookies, and the session cookie carries no platform info. References: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:88`, `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:90`.
- The GitLab research documents the generic connect handler sequence: feature flag check, IP rate limit, session requirement, client-id env validation, CSRF cookie creation, platform callback redirect URI construction, and redirect to platform authorization. Reference: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:39`.
- The GitLab research documents the generic callback sequence: flag check, rate limit, session, code/state/client-secret validation, token exchange, user fetch, `dbUpsertLinkedPlatform`, cache invalidation, state cookie clear, and redirect. Reference: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:47`.
- The GitLab research documents the generic disconnect sequence: POST, flag check, rate limit, session, `dbDeleteLinkedPlatform`, invalidation of merged stats, per-platform stats, and supplemental cache, then JSON success. Reference: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:50`.
- The GitLab research documents the generic status sequence: soft flag check, high rate limit, `dbGetLinkedPlatforms(session.login)`, and response with enabled, linked, remoteLogin, and connectedAt. Reference: `docs/research/2026-06-19-gitlab-integration.md:53`.
